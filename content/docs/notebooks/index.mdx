Jupyter Notebooks gives you a dedicated NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU node running JupyterLab. A workspace Admin or Owner allocates a node from the console and gets a Jupyter URL that includes an access token.

<Callout type="warn" title="Allocation is currently unavailable">
Notebook allocation is currently unavailable in the console, which shows RTX PRO 6000 notebooks as **At Capacity**. [Contact the Helios team](https://www.helios.co/contact) for access.
</Callout>

## How does it work?

- Notebooks are **admin-allocated**. A workspace Admin or Owner allocates a node. Members of the workspace can see the page but cannot allocate, refresh or deallocate.
- Each user can hold one active notebook at a time.
- After allocation, the console shows your Jupyter URL. You open it in a browser to use JupyterLab. The URL contains an access token.
- You can refresh the notebook to rotate its token, download your workspace, and deallocate it when you are done.
- Notebook storage is temporary. Download anything you want to keep before you stop using a notebook. See [Use a Jupyter notebook](/docs/notebooks/use-jupyter).

## Who is this for?

Teams that want an interactive GPU environment in the browser for development and experiments. For SSH access to a full 8-GPU node, see [Bare metal](/docs/bare-metal). For API-based model use, see [AI inference](/docs/inference).

## Pricing

Notebook pricing is shown on the card in the console. Confirm billing terms for your workspace with your Helios contact before you plan a long-running workload.
